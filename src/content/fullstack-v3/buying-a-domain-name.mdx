A **registrar** is a company that registers a domain to you as its owner —
Namecheap, Google Domains, and many others. The course uses **Namecheap**, not
because the UI is great but because it has the widest selection at the lowest
prices and doesn't push add-ons at you the whole way through.

You don't need any of the extras at checkout. **SSL you'll set up yourself, for
free**, later in the course.

<Callout label="Lock the account down">
Your registrar account is the single point of failure for your entire domain.
Steal those credentials and you can point the name anywhere — clone the site,
put up a login form, harvest everything. Strong password, two-factor, and as few
people with access as possible.
</Callout>

## Not every TLD is for sale

Some are **restricted** and you simply can't have them:

| | |
|---|---|
| `.org` | a registered organisation, typically a nonprofit |
| `.edu` | a legitimate educational institution |
| `.gov` | government entities |
| `.mil` | military |

Others are **country-level**, registered to that country with ICANN — `.ru` is
Russia's. But a country can sell the rights, which is how `.ly` ended up
carrying `bit.ly` and `fast.ly` during the vowel-dropping startup era. A small
island nation made real money because its country code happened to rhyme with
English.

Part of every purchase and renewal goes to **ICANN**, which is what funds the
nameserver system you've been using this whole time.

## Then hook it up

Buying the name is where most people stop, and it's the missing link: the domain
does nothing until you point it at something. Next you'll change the
**nameservers** to your host's and add an **A record** pointing the name at your
droplet's IP.

One rule for the name itself, and Jem is serious about it: make it funny or
memorable, ideally both.
